# Green Falls search and content review — October 2, 2026

## Assessment

The site can compete for specific service and buyer-question searches, but the old copy gave several pages too little subject detail. Titles such as “Services” and “Insights” weren't descriptive; “Practical AI” and “Marketing direction” under-described the offer. Service introductions were also being reused as long search descriptions. The article collection contained no email-specific guide, despite email/lifecycle being the strongest documented experience.

This is a search-intent and content audit, not a ranking report. No new Search Console or keyword-volume export was available during this work. Research confirmed relevant competing pages and recurring buyer questions; it did not establish search volume, keyword difficulty, current rankings or expected traffic. Treat the targets below as hypotheses to test with real impressions, queries and qualified inquiries.

Broad terms such as “Maine web design” have established competitors. A content update alone does not establish authority or guarantee a first-page result. More specific contractor, retailer, email-setup and buyer-question searches give each page a clearer purpose. Genuine reviews, relevant local links and demonstrated expertise remain important gaps. The owner does not want portfolio examples or invented proof added.

## Buyer questions addressed

- What does a small business website cost, and what changes the quote?
- Can someone improve the current site instead of replacing it?
- Is SEO included, and can a redesign affect existing rankings?
- Why aren't website visits becoming inquiries?
- What happens after someone requests a quote?
- Can Green Falls build or repair Klaviyo flows and also write/design campaigns?
- Which automated emails should come first?
- Can an independent shop without ecommerce still use customer email?
- What can AI do in a small business, and when is simple automation enough?
- What information does an AI workflow need, who checks it and what does setup include?
- Should a business repair its website before buying advertising?
- Can consultation begin with one decision rather than a retainer?

Pricing answers describe scope factors and how to get a quote. No fabricated starting prices, turnaround promises or ROI claims were introduced.

## Changes implemented

- Descriptive, distinct titles and concise search descriptions for primary pages, services, industries and articles.
- Service headings and introductions identify the actual service and audience.
- Four service pages expanded with useful scope, platform, measurement and buying information.
- Added three buyer FAQs to each service page; visible FAQ answers and schema use the same data.
- Expanded the website-inquiry guide to distinguish traffic problems from conversion problems.
- Added an original email-automation guide and a website-redesign proposal checklist.
- Added relevant service/article and audience/article links; corrected homepage guide labels.
- Article dates are explicit data used by visible dates, schema and sitemap. New guides are dated October 2; existing revised guides show an update date.
- Canonicals, current URLs, contact delivery, analytics and the favicon compatibility fix preserved.
- No new city doorway pages, hidden keyword copy, fabricated reviews, portfolio work or claims of guaranteed AI-search visibility.

## Technical interpretation

Google can rewrite title links and choose a snippet from visible content instead of the supplied description. Length checks are editorial aids, not ranking thresholds. Unique and accurate titles/descriptions matter more than hitting a universal character count.

FAQ markup is retained for accurate machine-readable content, not presented as a rich-result or ranking promise. AI search uses the same core requirements around crawlable, useful content; the site doesn't promise mentions from an AI tool or special ranking benefits from `llms.txt`.

## After publication

Do not request recrawling until the owner is ready. Once requested, compare Search Console impressions, queries, click-through and landing pages over meaningful periods. Track qualified inquiries separately from all form submissions and exclude tests. Expect small early samples; do not label a ranking or conversion improvement from a handful of visits.

Next evidence-building work: genuine reviews from actual clients, accurate business listings, relevant regional links, and further articles based on real sales questions. Never manufacture client results to fill those gaps.
